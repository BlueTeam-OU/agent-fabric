## 2. The law it was checked against

Read in this session, 2026-11-11:

- **MiCA**, Regulation (EU) 2023/1114, OJ L 150, 9.6.2023, p. 40, the
  English text as published in the Official Journal, taken from the
  Publications Office (CELEX 32023R1114). Article 3(1): point (5)
  defines "crypto-asset", point (10) "issuer" ("a natural or legal
  person, or other undertaking, who issues crypto-assets"), point (15)
  "crypto-asset service provider", and point (16) "crypto-asset
  service", whose point (a) is "providing custody and administration of
  crypto-assets on behalf of clients" and point (b) "operation of a
  trading platform for crypto-assets". The page's existing references
  are right; the redraft keeps them and adds point (10) beside "issue".
  Points (c) "exchange of crypto-assets for funds", (d) "exchange of
  crypto-assets for other crypto-assets", (e) "execution of orders for
  crypto-assets on behalf of clients" and (g) "reception and
  transmission of orders for crypto-assets on behalf of clients" are
  cited beside the page's "exchange or a broker" since the native read
  (section 6).
  Point (h) "providing advice on crypto-assets" and its definition in
  Article 3(1)(24) ("offering, giving or agreeing to give personalised
  recommendations to a client [...] in respect of one or more
  transactions relating to crypto-assets, or the use of crypto-asset
  services"), and Article 3(1)(14) "funds", which refers to Directive
  (EU) 2015/2366 Article 4 point (25) ("banknotes and coins, scriptural
  money or electronic money"; CELLAR 32015L2366, OJ text, read
  2026-11-11). The Estonian version of MiCA renders "funds" as "raha"
  (money) and point (h) as "krüptovara kohta nõu andmine" (CELLAR
  32023R1114, Accept-Language est, read 2026-11-11).
  Later amendments of MiCA were not checked; none is needed for these
  definitions to be read as the page uses them.
- **Law of Obligations Act** (võlaõigusseadus, VÕS), Riigi Teataja,
  consolidated text in force 01.09.2026 to 31.12.2026, RT I, 20.06.2026,
  18, Estonian text:
  - § 37(1): standard terms (tüüptingimused) are part of a contract only
    if the user clearly referred to them as part of the contract before
    or when it was concluded and the other party could learn their
    content. A page on the website that the contract does not refer to
    is not part of the contract.
  - § 38: a term the parties agreed separately prevails over a standard
    term that contradicts it.
  - § 106(1)–(2): parties may agree in advance to exclude or limit
    liability, but an agreement that excludes or limits liability for an
    intentional breach (tahtlik rikkumine), or that otherwise
    unreasonably excludes or limits it, is void.
  - § 42(3) point 1, with § 44: a standard term that excludes or limits
    liability for death or harm to health, or for damage caused
    intentionally or through gross negligence (raske hooletus), is
    unfair to a consumer, and presumed unfair to a business.

## 3. Reading

The page informs; it binds no one by itself. Under VÕS § 37(1) its
liability paragraph becomes a contract term only where a contract refers
to it, and then § 38, § 42 and § 106 apply to it as to any standard
term. The owner's sentence that the contract prevails states that
honestly and matches § 38; it adds meaning to the page, which is why it
was the owner's decision.

The exceptions the page already states (gross negligence, wilful
misconduct, and where the law does not allow liability to be limited)
cover what § 106(2) and § 42(3) point 1 with § 44 reach. The redraft
keeps them as they are. It does not name death or personal injury
separately: "where the law does not allow it" already reaches them, and
the owner asked that the meaning stay.

A client's actual protection is in the client contract, which is the
subject of a separate matter (the consultant agreement review, j23).

## 4. Conditions the text holds under

- BlueTeam issues no crypto-assets, holds no crypto-assets of others,
  and runs no trading platform; it accepts Bitcoin as payment only.
  (The page says so today; not re-verified with the owner here.)
- BlueTeam gives no client personalised recommendations on transactions
  in crypto-assets or on which crypto-asset services or providers to use
  (MiCA Article 3(1)(24)); its advice is on the technology. The owner
  confirmed that the company does not recommend to a client which
  crypto-asset services or providers to use (owner, in session,
  2026-11-11, relayed by brand-comms-01 in REQUEST 01a1cc24). If that
  changes, the point (h) sentence is wrong and the question comes back
  to this role.
- The AI Act conditions of `2026-11-09-ai-act-claims-blueteam-ee.md`
  and of the owner's answers of 2026-11-10 still hold.
- BlueTeam does not put its name on a client's high-risk AI system,
  substantially modify one, or change the intended purpose of a
  client's AI system (AI Act Article 25(1)(a) to (c)); otherwise it
  becomes the provider, and the AI section is wrong. The owner has
  not been asked to state this; brand-comms-01 put the offer of a
  clause on the page to the owner on 2026-11-12, advising that the
  client contract carry it instead.
- If BlueTeam ever offers a service to consumers, the liability
  paragraph is read again against VÕS § 42(3) before that service
  starts.

## 6. After the native read (2026-11-11)

language-culture-en read the page as placed (blueteam.ee ab968c4), and
brand-comms put seven spans to this role (REQUEST 01a1cbdc). Accepted:
"In the terms used by", spelled-out ICO and STO, the Bitcoin

"tort" stays: it is the term of art, and "whatever the legal basis"
and "or otherwise" carry the meaning. None of this changes what the
page excludes or limits.

A second read (REQUEST 01a1cbe0) asked three more things of the MiCA
paragraph. "We are also not" replaces the inverted "Nor are we".
"funds (money)" glosses MiCA's term, as its Estonian version does. And
the list of services is now marked as examples of a whole claim (none
of the services in Article 3(1)(16)), with a sentence on point (h),
because the page does give advice and a closed list invited the reading
that the advice is MiCA advice. That sentence holds only while the
company gives no personalised recommendations on transactions in
crypto-assets or on which crypto-asset services a client should use;
the first is the page's "Not investment advice", the second is a fact
the owner confirmed on 2026-11-11 (section 4).

A third read (REQUEST 01a1cc24) found that "which is what the
Regulation calls ..." could attach to "the use of crypto-asset
services" alone. Article 3(1)(24) defines advice on crypto-assets as
personalised recommendations on both objects, transactions and the use
of services (section 2), so the clause is now its own sentence, with
the defined term in quotation marks. language-culture-en's span keeps
every word; it changes no exclusion on the page.

## Scope of our services

BlueTeam OÜ is a technical consultancy. This page sets out plainly what
we do and what we don't, so you know what to expect before we work
together.

### At a glance

- We give technical advice and help you build and run technology.
- We do not give investment, legal, accounting or tax advice.
- We do not issue crypto-assets, hold them for others, or run a
  platform for trading them.
- We use AI tools in our work. Every deliverable produced with AI is
  reviewed independently, and a BlueTeam consultant is responsible for
  it.

### Not investment advice

We help you make technical decisions. We may discuss market or industry
trends where they affect a technical choice, but we do not advise on
investment strategies, asset allocation or other financial matters. For
that, please consult a qualified financial advisor.

### Not legal, accounting or tax advice

Anything we say about law, accounting or tax is general information, not
professional advice. For legal matters, please consult a licensed
attorney; for accounting, a certified accountant; for tax, a licensed
tax professional.

### Crypto-assets: technical advice only

We can advise you on the technology of tokens and other crypto-assets,
and help you implement them. We do not:

- issue tokens or other crypto-assets, or run initial coin offerings
  (ICOs), security token offerings (STOs) or any other token-based
  fundraising;
- hold or manage crypto-assets for anyone else (we accept Bitcoin as
  payment, but the only crypto-assets we hold are our own);
- run a platform for buying and selling crypto-assets, or act as an
  exchange or a broker.

We do not guarantee that any token or crypto-asset will succeed. Our work
for a client, whether or not it is public, does not mean we endorse the
client's token, its technology or its token economics.

In the terms used by the EU Markets in Crypto-Assets Regulation
([Regulation (EU) 2023/1114, MiCA](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/1114/oj)),
we are not an issuer of crypto-assets (Article 3(1)(5) and (10)). We
are also not a crypto-asset service provider (Article 3(1)(15)): we
provide none of the crypto-asset services listed in Article 3(1)(16).
For example, we do not provide custody and administration of
crypto-assets on behalf of clients (point (a)), operate a trading
platform for crypto-assets (point (b)), exchange crypto-assets for
funds (money) or for other crypto-assets (points (c) and (d)), or
execute, receive or transmit orders for crypto-assets on behalf of
clients (points (e) and (g)). Our advice is on the technology of
crypto-assets. We do not give a client personalised recommendations on
transactions in crypto-assets or on the use of crypto-asset services.
The Regulation calls such recommendations "advice on crypto-assets"
(point (h) and Article 3(1)(24)).

### How we use AI

We use AI tools built on AI models from other companies. Here is what
that means for you:

- every deliverable produced with AI goes through an independent
  review, and a BlueTeam OÜ consultant is responsible for that
  deliverable;
- we tell you when we use AI in your engagement, and if the law
  requires us to disclose that content was made or changed by AI, we
  do so;
- we do not offer general-purpose AI models, we do not offer or put
  into use high-risk AI systems under our own name, and we do not
  build or use AI for any purpose the law prohibits;
- if we work on a high-risk AI system that you offer or put into use
  under your own name, you are responsible for it as its provider, and
  we help you as agreed in writing.

This section describes how we work. It is not advice on your own
obligations under the AI Act (see "Not legal, accounting or tax
advice" above).

In the terms used by the EU Artificial Intelligence Act
([Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, the AI Act](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj)),
BlueTeam OÜ is a deployer of AI systems built on general-purpose AI
models from third-party providers. We do not place a general-purpose
AI model on the market, and we do not place on the market or put into
service a high-risk AI system of our own. We do not develop or use AI
systems for any practice prohibited by Article 5 of the Act, and we do
not develop high-risk AI systems listed in its Annex III, for
ourselves or for clients. Where we integrate or advise on a client's
system that is high-risk, the client is its provider if the system is
placed on the market or put into service under the client's name, and
we give the client the information and assistance agreed in writing
for the client to meet its obligations. Where Article 50 of the Act
requires us to disclose that content has been artificially generated
or manipulated, we do so.

### Limits of our liability

As far as Estonian and European Union law allow, we are not liable for
loss or damage that arises from our services or advice, or from our
inability to provide them. That includes loss of data, loss of
profits, business interruption, and indirect or consequential loss.

This applies whatever the legal basis of a claim: contract, tort
(including negligence) or otherwise. It does not apply in cases of
gross negligence or wilful misconduct, or where the law does not allow
liability to be limited.

Where you have a contract with us, that contract sets out our liability;
where the contract and this page differ, the contract applies.
