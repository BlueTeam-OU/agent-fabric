This policy explains how BlueTeam OÜ uses personal data when you contact
us, become our client, or visit this website.

### 1. Who we are

The controller of your personal data is **BlueTeam OÜ**, registry code
14454093, Narva mnt 5, 10117 Tallinn, Estonia, `info@blueteam.ee`.

Questions about your data go to our data protection contact,
[FACT: name, as on the current page], at the same address. We have not

### 2. What we use, why, and for how long

| When | Personal data | Why | Legal basis | How long |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| You contact us | name, email, phone, company, job title, your message | to answer you, and to prepare an offer if you ask for one | our legitimate interest in answering (Art. 6(1)(f)); steps before a contract at your request (Art. 6(1)(b)) | [FACT: proposed 24 months] after our last exchange, unless it leads to a contract |

We use no cookies and no analytics or tracking on this website.

**Work in your systems.** When we work in a client's systems, we may see
personal data held there, such as logs, screenshots or settings. We
process it only on that client's behalf and on its instructions, under
our agreement with the client. The client's own privacy notice applies
to that data, not this one.

### 3. Whether you have to give us your data

Nothing obliges you to contact us. To make and perform a contract we
need the contact and billing details in the table above, and the law
requires them on our invoices. Without them we cannot work with you.

We also disclose data where the law, a court or an authority requires
it, and to our legal advisers where we need advice.

### 5. AI tools

We use AI tools from Anthropic and OpenAI in our work.

- **Anthropic (Claude).** We do not enter your personal data, or
  personal data we handle for our clients, into it. Where a task needs a
  log, a screenshot or a document that contains personal data, we remove
  the personal data first. Anthropic receives no personal data from us,
  and is not among the recipients in section 4.
- **OpenAI (business plan).** OpenAI processes what we enter on our
  instructions, under a data processing agreement, and does not use it
  to train its models [FACT: confirm in the account that the data
  processing agreement is accepted and that training is off]. It may
  keep a copy for abuse monitoring in the United States (section 6).

Personal data we handle for a client goes into an AI tool only where
that client has agreed to it in writing.

We make no decisions about you by automated means, and we do not
profile you.

### 6. Transfers outside the EEA

Our providers keep your data in the EEA or in Switzerland
[FACT: confirm that Smart Accounts hosts its data in the EEA; its
privacy policy does not say]. Under EU law, Switzerland is covered by
an adequacy decision of the European Commission. The one exception is OpenAI's
copy for abuse monitoring in the United States (sections 4 and 5),
covered by [FACT: the safeguard OpenAI's data processing agreement
states — the EU-US Data Privacy Framework or Standard Contractual
Clauses]. You can ask us for a copy of the safeguard at
`info@blueteam.ee`. We do not transfer your data to any other country.
What Swiss law says of these transfers is in section 10.

### 7. Social media

We have a page on LinkedIn. LinkedIn and we are joint controllers of the
statistics LinkedIn gives us about visits to our page. LinkedIn publishes
the essence of that arrangement [link: LinkedIn's Page Insights Joint
Controller Addendum], and you can exercise your rights against either of
us. Anything else you send us through LinkedIn or Telegram is also
processed by those platforms under their own privacy policies.

### 8. Your rights

You can ask us to:

- give you a copy of your data (the first copy is free);
- correct it, or complete it;
- erase it, or restrict how we use it, where the law allows;
- give it to you, or to another organisation, in a machine-readable form,
  where we use it under a contract or your consent.

Where we rely on consent, you can withdraw it at any time; that does not
affect what we did before.

We answer within one month. For complex or numerous requests we may take
two more months, and if so we tell you within the first month. If you
are in Switzerland, section 10 says what Swiss law adds to this.

> **Your right to object.** Where we use your data on the basis of our
> legitimate interests (section 2), you can object at any time, for
> reasons that concern your particular situation. We then stop, unless we
> show compelling legitimate grounds that override your interests, or we
> need the data for a legal claim. Write to `info@blueteam.ee`.

### 9. Complaints

You can complain to the Estonian Data Protection Inspectorate
(Andmekaitse Inspektsioon), Tatari 39, 10134 Tallinn, `info@aki.ee`,
or to the supervisory authority where you live or work in the EEA.

If you are in Switzerland, you can also report a breach of Swiss data
protection law to the Federal Data Protection and Information
Commissioner (FDPIC), Feldeggweg 1, 3003 Bern, Switzerland,
www.edoeb.admin.ch.

### 10. If you are in Switzerland

Where the Swiss Federal Act on Data Protection applies to our use of
your data, the following also applies:

- Switzerland's Federal Council recognises Estonia, Finland, Iceland
  and Ireland as countries with adequate data protection. For OpenAI's
  copy in the United States, [FACT: either "OpenAI is certified under
  the Swiss-U.S. Data Privacy Framework", or the safeguard OpenAI's
  data processing agreement gives for Swiss data].
- We answer a request for a copy of your data within 30 days, free of
  charge. If we need longer, we tell you within those 30 days and say
  when you will have it. We may ask you to share the cost, up to
  CHF 300, only where answering takes disproportionate effort, and we
  tell you the amount before we start.
- We have not appointed a representative in Switzerland, because the
  Act does not require one for our processing.

### 11. Changes

We may update this policy. The date at the top shows the current
version. Where a change affects how we use data you have already given
us, we tell you before it takes effect.

## Removed from the current page, and why

- The paragraph claiming the record-of-processing exemption
  (Article 30(5)): the exemption is unlikely to apply, and a notice is
  not the place for it.
- The data breach section: it promised more than Articles 33(1) and
  34(1) require, and Article 13 does not ask for it.
- "We do not collect any data from your visit": the host keeps server
  logs (now in section 2).
- "At your own risk" for social media: it does not discharge the joint
  controllership (now section 7).
- The glossary: it is not required, and its "privacy by default" entry
  was wrong. It can come back, corrected, if brand-comms wants it.
