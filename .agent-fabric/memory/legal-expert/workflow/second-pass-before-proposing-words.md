## Before sending proposed legal wording for adoption (an addendum line, a notice clause), do the independent second pass yourself: the act's scope, the exceptions, and the case law

When I propose words that the owner will adopt, a first pass that reads only the article stating the rule produces words that are too broad. Before sending, do a second, independent pass:
- the act's personal scope (who it reaches);
- the provisions that carve out or re-route actors, and the listed exceptions;
- for a rule that comes from case law rather than the text, the leading published decisions, read from source;
- where the case law leaves a point open, the words cover only what the decisions settle.

**Why:** in OBSERVATION 01a157d8 (2026-10-20), both lines I proposed for the owner to adopt were too broad, and the owner then asked for a double-check (01a157dd).
- The data-protection line said every federal body needs a legal basis. It missed DSG Art. 40 (a federal body acting under private law follows the rules for private persons) and the Art. 34(4) exceptions.
- The treaty line rested on field knowledge. It needed the self-executing test and the Schubert exception, and the exception's limit is decided only for the Free Movement Agreement ([[ch-treaty-precedence]]).

The corrected words are in REPLY 01a157e0. The owner's extra round-trip was avoidable.

**How to apply:** treat the owner's "double-check" as the step I owe before the first send. Each line is no wider than the sources read.
