## For directive copy (band, notification, hint), the first pass asks what the reader does next; a gap goes to the meaning's owner, never invented

On the call-sign copy (gzapp #1119, ADR-078) the cold read asked "so what do I do?" four times running: the rider hearing the sign; the driver with no sign; a driver at the wheel told to "look for the rider aboard" (impossible); a push body with no action, read as "open the app". Each time I did not invent an action but raised it as a point for the meaning's owner in the REPLY, and twice architect-cto amended ADR-078 on it (an action clause in the hint; "ask who is getting off" instead of "look aboard", which is what a marshrutka driver does). For copy that tells someone to act, ask on the first pass what the reader does after reading it and whether they can do it where they are; a gap or an impossible act is a point for the meaning's owner, with local practice if known, and the copy ships as requested.
