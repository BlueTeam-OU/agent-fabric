## A partner's or venue's name on a brand surface — check it against the institution's own pages in each language; a name from memory can belong to a different site of the same institution

The name of an institution, agency or venue that a deck or page addresses is a fact, and it is checked against that institution's own pages: its own site, and its About page in each language the surface carries. It is never taken from the deck that went before, from memory, or from a reviewer's grammar.

**Why:** a deck addressed a national agency's regional venue by the English name "Technopark" for two releases. The venue's own English site calls it a Technology Hub. "Technopark" was the name of the agency's *other* facility, in a different city, so the slide placed the team in the wrong city for the audience that knew both. The same deck's Georgian form of the agency's name was flagged twice by native readers as ungrammatical. It was closed by the agency's own spelling of its own name, not by the grammar rule: a proper name is spelled as its owner spells it.

**How to apply:** for each third-party name, cite the page that confirms it, per language, before release. Decide which kind of problem a mismatch is:
- **A matter of taste:** a short form, or a name the institution does not use for itself. Put it to the owner with the evidence.
- **An error:** the name of a different entity or site. Fix it, tell the owner what changed and on whose evidence, and revert if they know better.
