## Swiss law on a treaty provision against a federal act: the self-executing test, Art. 190 BV, precedence of international law, the Schubert exception and its limits (FZA, human rights)

Read from source 2026-10-20 on relevancy.bger.ch ([[bger-relevancy-host]]), with BV SR 101 from Fedlex (Stand 3.3.2024).
- **Self-executing:** a treaty provision applies directly when it is "inhaltlich hinreichend bestimmt und klar ..., um im Einzelfall Grundlage eines Entscheides zu bilden", is justiciable, concerns individuals' rights and duties, and is addressed to the authorities that apply the law (BGE 124 III 90 E. 3a).
- **Art. 190 BV** makes federal acts and international law both "massgebend" and does not resolve a conflict (BGE 125 II 417 E. 4d, on its predecessor). The court resolves one by pacta sunt servanda (Art. 5(4) BV; Art. 26/27 VCLT): international law prevails in principle, also over a later federal act, and the contrary national rule is not applied in the case (BGE 125 II 417 E. 4d; BGE 139 I 16 E. 5.1).
- **Schubert exception** (BGE 99 Ib 39 E. 3-4, in Italian): a federal act prevails where the legislature knowingly accepted the conflict. It does not hold against human-rights treaties (BGE 125 II 417 E. 4d; BGE 139 I 16 E. 5.1). It also does not hold against the Free Movement Agreement (BGE 142 II 35 E. 3.2, confirming BGE 133 V 367, which I have not read).
- **Open:** no decision I have read says whether Schubert holds against other bilateral agreements, and I have read none later than 2015.

**How to apply:** when a bilateral agreement may govern a Swiss question, first test whether the provision is self-executing; never write that it "binds through the Swiss act".
