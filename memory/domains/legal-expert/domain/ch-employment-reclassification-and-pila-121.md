## Swiss test for reclassifying a consultant as an employee (Art. 319 CO, BGE), and why a choice of the employer's law holds before a Swiss court (PILA Art. 121(3), no favourability); read 2026-11-11

Read from source on 2026-11-11 for j20 (REPLY 01a1cae3 to legal-expert-ee-eu; opinion off-repo by the owner's choice).

**Reclassification** (settled):
- An employment contract is work "im Dienst des Arbeitgebers" for a wage. Per-task pay (Akkordlohn) is included: Art. 319(1) CO, SR 220, status 1 Oct 2026.
- The label does not count; the real intention governs: Art. 18(1) CO; BGE 129 III 664 E. 3.1.
- The decisive criterion is subordination, i.e. receiving instructions: BGE 130 III 213 E. 2.1; 112 II 41 E. 1a (FR).
- The indicators are in 129 III 664 E. 3.2 (FR): freedom over one's work and time, instructions, periodic reports, a hierarchy, the business risk, own infrastructure, a commercial register entry.
- These BGE are on relevancy.bger.ch as `atf://129-III-664:de` etc.

**Conflict of laws before a Swiss court:**
- PILA Art. 121(3) (SR 291, status 1 Jan 2026; DE/FR/IT checked) allows choosing the law of the employee's residence or of the employer's establishment.
- There is no favourability comparison, unlike Rome I Art. 8. A validly chosen foreign law displaces Art. 361/362 CO. Only Art. 18 PILA (mandatory rules) is left, and which CO rules qualify I have not yet read in doctrine. That is a gap to fill before relying on it.
- Lugano Art. 19–21 and Art. 23(5) protect the employee's forum.

**FADP over a foreign controller:**
- Art. 3(1) is an effects test (DE "auswirken", FR "déploient des effets", IT "generano effetti").
- Under Art. 3(2), private-law claims go through PILA Art. 139(3), with the victim's choice of the law of their residence.

**Open, not read:**
- AHV status independent of the civil-law classification;
- the Labour Act's reach over a foreign employer;
- FDPIC guidance on Art. 3.
