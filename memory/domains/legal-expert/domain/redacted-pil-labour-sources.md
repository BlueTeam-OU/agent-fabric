## Georgian PIL, labour-classification and judgment-recognition points read on Matsne 2026-11-11 (choice of law, court clauses, reciprocity, employee test)

Read on Matsne in the Georgian original on 2026-11-11 (job j29). Each act is the latest consolidated version.

**PIL Law (Matsne 93712, version of 15/12/2023):**
- Art 6: Georgian imperative norms apply whatever law governs the contract.
- Art 35(3): a choice of law is void if it disregards the imperative norms of the most closely connected country.
- Art 36(1): the most closely connected country is presumed to be the residence of the characteristic performer.
- Art 38: a choice of law is void if it disregards norms protecting consumers and workers. This also covers labour **or service** contracts agreed in the worker's country of residence.
- Art 18(3): a foreign court clause is allowed if one party resides abroad.
- Art 18(4): court clauses are void only for the matters in Arts 10–16; labour is not among them.
- Art 68(2): grounds for refusing recognition of a foreign judgment. Ground (e) is **reciprocity**. Art 68(3) waives it only for non-property decisions.
- Arts 68(5) and 70(3): the Supreme Court decides recognition and enforcement.

**Labour Code (Matsne 1155567, version of 16/09/2026):**
- Art 2(1): the test is "შრომის ორგანიზაციული მოწესრიგების პირობებში" (work done under the employer's organisation of work).
- There is no statutory presumption of employment.
- Art 2(4)–(7) were deleted on 29.09.2020.

**Civil Code (Matsne 31702, version of 25/06/2026):**
- Art 52: a declaration of will is interpreted by reasonable judgment, not by its literal words.
- Art 56(2): a disguised transaction is governed by the rules of the transaction it covers.
