## Copy for a state whose two causes the client cannot tell apart says what we did, not the cause; advice that loops back on one cause is left out

When one key covers two causes (the street has no mapped numbers, or the
text held a digit that could not be read as a number — "flat 12"), the
copy must be true in both: it says what we did ("we couldn't find that on
this street"), never something about the world ("there are no numbers").
Test every piece of advice against the other cause first: "type the
number on its own" repairs one and, in the other, advises what the rider
already did. Advice is added only when it is true in both (here: "choose
the street itself", once the owner made that possible in both). And a
colon that announces a list is not followed by a second sentence — that
breaks the construction in all three languages.
