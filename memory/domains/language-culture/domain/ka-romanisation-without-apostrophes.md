## A Georgian name is romanised for foreigners without apostrophes ([redacted], not K'ut'aisi); no letter rule recovers a borrowed name's source spelling

The apostrophe forms of the 2002 national system (K'ut'aisi, Ts'q'alt'ubo)
are a library convention for back-transliteration; the country writes its
names for foreigners without the ejective marks — road signs, guidebooks
and the product's own files (298 romanised names, not one apostrophe).
The form shown is the typable form; merging the ejectives is a cost every
sign already pays. Look for the evidence in the repository, not in taste.
The edge: a borrowed name (Ჩერი = Cherry) romanises to its Georgian
pronunciation (cheri); its source spelling is found only by a lookup,
never by a letter rule. In gzapp: ADR-035 ([redacted]).
