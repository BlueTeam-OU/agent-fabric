## in legal copy, a list after a colon reads as complete; 'none of the X listed in Y. For example, ...' makes it an example; a trailing 'which is what ... calls' attaches to the nearest noun

A list after a colon ("we are not a provider: we do not do a, b or c") is read as complete. A reader then infers that we do d, the item left out. On the blueteam.ee disclaimer MiCA paragraph, legal-expert-ee-eu fixed this with "we provide none of the services listed in Article 3(1)(16). For example, we do not ..." (j283, REPLY 01a1cc24-5388, 2026-11-12). In the same paragraph, a closing ", which is what the Regulation calls X" could attach to the nearest noun only. Splitting it into a sentence ("The Regulation calls such recommendations 'X'") removes the doubt and changes no word of the claim.

**Why:** a second-language reader takes the nearest antecedent and the literal extent of a list.
**How to apply:** when a legal sentence lists exclusions after a colon, ask whether the list is meant to be complete. If not, propose a "none of ... For example" form and send it to the legal owner. Make a closing "which" clause its own sentence with a noun subject. Related: [[legal-condition-sentence-stays-whole]], [[mica-advice-on-crypto-assets-collision]].
